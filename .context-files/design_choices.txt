================================================================================
1. PROJECT CONTEXT
================================================================================

Restaurant Rush is a Java command-line, turn-based restaurant management
game developed for the SC2002 group assignment.

The assignment is implemented in three stages.

This document covers STAGE 1 ONLY.

Stage 1 contains:
- An 18-turn restaurant simulation
- Two tables
- One Waiter, one Chef and one Cashier
- Scheduled customer arrivals
- Seating, ordering, preparation, serving and payment
- Customer satisfaction and abandonment
- Victory and defeat evaluation

Stage 2 and Stage 3 features, including VIP customers, Critics, Hosts,
ComboMeals and Happy Hour, are outside the scope of this implementation.

The original UML divides the project into five work packages:
- Person 1: Core Game Logic
- Person 2: Customer and Order System
- Person 3: Staff and Restaurant Operations
- Person 4: Menu System
- Person 5: Command-Line Interface

The final UML must accurately reflect the implemented classes and
relationships, including appropriate object-oriented design principles.

================================================================================
2. TURN FLOW
================================================================================

The implemented game uses sequential decision processing.

Each turn follows this sequence:

1. GameController starts the turn.

2. A customer arrives if the turn number is:
   1, 4, 7, 10, 13 or 16.

3. The current restaurant state is displayed.

4. The manager selects a Waiter action:
   - WAIT
   - SEAT
   - TAKE_ORDER
   - SERVE

5. The Waiter choice is validated and, if valid, executed immediately.

6. The manager selects a Chef preparation action or skips preparation.

7. The Chef choice is validated and, if valid, executed immediately.

8. TurnProcessor performs automatic end-of-turn processing:
   - Eating progression
   - Eligible cashier payments
   - Satisfaction reduction
   - Customer abandonment

9. After turn 18, the game result is evaluated.

IMPORTANT TIMING RULES

- The Waiter performs at most one valid action per turn.
- The Chef performs at most one preparation unit per turn.
- The Cashier processes at most one eligible payment per turn.
- Invalid choices do not consume the relevant staff action.
- The Chef may prepare an order created earlier in the same turn.
- An order cannot be served on the turn it becomes READY.
- A customer cannot pay on the turn they become READY_TO_PAY.
- A customer served on turn S finishes eating on turn S+1 and
  can pay on turn S+2 at the earliest.

The implementation uses immediate validation and execution rather
than collecting all decisions and applying them as a batch.

================================================================================
3. CLASS DESIGN CHOICES
================================================================================

3.1 GameController

GameController coordinates the game.

Responsibilities:
- Start the game
- Start turns
- Process scheduled arrivals
- Validate and execute Waiter choices
- Validate and execute Chef choices
- End turns
- Delegate victory/defeat evaluation
- Expose GameState to the CLI

GameController owns GameState and TurnProcessor.

It maintains references to the staff objects, Menu and
CustomerChoiceGenerator.

Internal step and validation methods remain private.

The public action-execution methods return a String:
- null means the choice was valid and executed.
- A non-null String describes why the choice was rejected.

This enables the CLI to display useful error messages.

3.2 TurnProcessor

TurnProcessor is responsible for automatic end-of-turn processing.

Responsibilities:
- Progress customer eating
- Process eligible payments
- Reduce satisfaction where applicable
- Handle customer abandonment

The separation prevents GameController from becoming responsible
for every detail of the simulation.

Player-directed seating, ordering, serving and preparation remain
outside TurnProcessor.

3.3 Waiter choices

WaiterChoice is an abstract value-object class.

Its subclasses are:
- WaitChoice
- SeatChoice(customerId, tableId)
- TakeOrderChoice(tableId)
- ServeChoice(orderId)

Each choice carries only the identifiers required by its action.

This avoids using one ambiguous generic targetId.

The Chef selects an order using an integer orderId.

These choices support scripted and reproducible tests.

3.4 GameState

GameState stores the restaurant's shared simulation data.

It tracks:
- Current turn
- Revenue in integer cents
- Paid customer count
- Served customer count
- Unhappy departures
- Total satisfaction of paid customers
- Waiting customers
- All customers
- Orders
- Tables

Money is represented using integer cents to avoid floating-point
errors in financial calculations.

GameState evaluates the victory conditions.

Victory requires all of the following after turn 18:
- At least 4 paid customers
- At least 4500 cents in revenue
- Average paid-customer satisfaction of at least 60

GameState also provides detailed defeat causes.

Collection getters return read-only views to protect internal state.

When no customer has paid, the CLI displays average satisfaction
as N/A.

3.5 Customer

Customer stores:
- Customer identifier
- Satisfaction
- Customer status
- Served turn
- Ready-to-pay turn
- Associated order
- Assigned table

Customers begin with satisfaction 100.

Customer state changes generally occur through lifecycle methods.
The current implementation also has a public setStatus() method,
which TurnProcessor uses to mark abandoned customers as LEFT.

Relevant operations include:
- seatAt()
- assignOrder()
- decreaseSatisfaction()
- hasAbandoned()
- onServed()
- finishEating()
- onPaid()

Waiting or seated customers who have not been served lose
8 satisfaction points per turn, to a minimum of 0.

A customer who reaches 0 satisfaction before being served
leaves without paying.

Any unfinished order is cancelled and the occupied table,
if applicable, is freed.

3.6 Order

Order stores:
- Order identifier
- Immutable customer identifier
- Selected MenuItem
- Order status
- Remaining preparation units
- Final price in cents
- Turn when the order became READY

Order status changes occur through controlled methods:
- advance()
- markServed()
- markPaid()
- cancel()

An order may be served only after the turn it became READY.

The final price is initially the MenuItem price.

A separate final-price setter allows future pricing adjustments
without changing the MenuItem base price.

3.7 CustomerChoiceGenerator

CustomerChoiceGenerator selects food automatically.

The Stage 1 selection is deterministic:

  index = (customerId - 1) % menuSize

This produces reproducible choices for testing.

The manager does not choose food on behalf of customers.

3.8 Staff and tables

Waiter, Chef and Cashier are separate classes without a shared
staff superclass.

Waiter handles:
- Seating
- Taking orders
- Serving prepared food

Chef handles:
- Order preparation

Cashier handles:
- Eligible customer payments

Table stores:
- Table identifier
- Current occupant

Table operations include:
- occupy()
- free()
- isAvailable()

Customers also maintain a reference to their assigned table.

The restaurant contains two tables.

3.9 Menu

Menu contains the fixed Stage 1 dishes:

- Salad: 900 cents, 1 preparation unit
- Burger: 1500 cents, 1 preparation unit
- Pasta: 1800 cents, 2 preparation units

MenuItem stores:
- Name
- Base price in cents
- Required preparation units

MenuItem validates its constructor inputs.

MenuItem data is exposed through getters rather than public
mutable fields.

3.10 Command-line interface

The playable Stage 1 entry point is:

  game.RestaurantRushCLI

The CLI is responsible for:
- Displaying the restaurant state
- Collecting manager decisions
- Showing validation errors
- Displaying turn summaries
- Displaying final results

Game rules remain in the controller and domain classes.

The CLI depends on GameController.

GameController does not depend on the CLI.

The original UML names RestaurantCLI and Main separately.
The implemented entry-point structure is recorded as a deviation
in deviation_from_v1_s1_uml.txt.

4. TESTING AND VERIFICATION
================================================================================

Stage 1 includes automated regression and edge-case tests.

Relevant test classes include:
- ControllerSimulationTest
- Stage1RegressionTest
- Stage1EdgeCaseTest

The legacy Stage1TurnSequenceHarness is also retained.

The regression and edge-case suites have passed 21 tests in total.

Verified scenarios include:
- Scheduled arrivals
- Seating and ordering
- Staff action limits
- Invalid choice handling
- Order preparation
- Serving restrictions
- Eating and payment timing
- Table reuse
- Customer abandonment
- Read-only collection exposure
- Exactly 18 turns
- Victory and defeat outcomes

The playable CLI has also been compiled and launched successfully.
